# The Complete Buyer's Guide to Wyers End in White Salmon, WA

![Wyers End Home](/wyers-end/wyers-end-photo-2.jpg)

Nestled in the heart of White Salmon, Wyers End is one of the most unique and sought-after Planned Unit Developments (PUDs) in the Columbia River Gorge. With its dense, community-focused layout and easy access to town, it offers a fantastic lifestyle.

However, buying a home in a highly regulated PUD means you are buying into a very specific set of rules. As a local real estate expert, I always want my buyers to know exactly what is under the hood before they write an offer.

Here is everything you need to know about buying, living, and thriving in Wyers End.

![Wyers End Landscaping](/wyers-end/wyers-end-photo.jpg)

## Neighborhood History & Overview

The Wyers End subdivision was officially approved by the White Salmon City Council in early 2007. The developers designed it as a mixed-use residential community with a highly specific footprint to maximize space and create a tight-knit neighborhood feel.  

The community consists of exactly 28 lots, broken down into three distinct styles of homes:

* 10 Townhouses   
* 11 Bungalows   
* 7 Cottages   

*(Note: Lots 19 through 28 were annexed in later phases and have slightly different rules allowing for "low impact" commercial uses like home offices.)*

## Crucial Financials: The Transfer Fee

When budgeting for your closing costs, there is a specific Wyers End fee you need to be prepared for.

* At the closing of any resale in the neighborhood, the purchaser is required to pay a one-time contribution to the HOA's working capital.  
* This fee is legally defined as one-sixth (1/6) of the annual HOA assessment for the lot you are purchasing (often estimated around $200, depending on the current budget).  
* This is not a prepayment of your regular dues; it is a separate capital injection to keep the HOA reserves healthy.  

## 3 Things You Should Know

If you are moving to the Gorge, you likely have outdoor toys, a desire for flexibility, and an eye on investment potential. Here is how Wyers End regulates those aspects:

**1. The "Gorge Lifestyle" Parking Ban**
This is the number one dealbreaker for some buyers. The CC&Rs strictly prohibit the parking of boats, trailers, commercial vehicles, motor homes, and campers. They cannot be parked or even left unattended on your lot, the common areas, or even inside your garage at any time. Furthermore, parking is heavily restricted to designated reserved spaces; if you have multiple vehicles or frequent guests, parking will be tight.  

**2. No Short-Term Rentals (No Airbnb)**
If you are hoping to offset your mortgage by renting out a spare room on weekends, Wyers End is not the neighborhood for you. The HOA mandates that any rentals must be for a period of not less than six months. If you do sign a long-term lease with a tenant, you are also required to post a maintenance bond with the Association.  

**3. The "City Hammer" Clause**
Wyers End features private roads (like Upper Wyers and Fifth Place). To ensure the City of White Salmon wouldn't get stuck paying to plow or pave them, the HOA signed a perpetual "Subdivision Improvement Maintenance Agreement".  

* The HOA is legally required to keep the roads cleared of snow and maintained to City standards.  
* If the HOA fails to do this, the City has the legal authority to step in, fix the roads, and bill the HOA directly—even applying a 12% interest rate for late payments. As a buyer, this is why we must rigorously review the HOA's financial health during your contingency period.  

## Upper Wyers Additional Info: The Dedicated Parking Amenity

For buyers looking specifically at the Upper Wyers phase (Lots 19-28), there is a unique and highly convenient amenity to be aware of. 

Because parking within the main community footprint is so strictly regulated, the HOA owns and maintains a dedicated overflow parking lot just to the north of this phase. This exclusive lot is a massive benefit for homeowners who have additional vehicles or frequent guests, providing breathing room that the rest of the neighborhood doesn't have. 

To maintain this premium space, there is a small additional HOA assessment tied to these specific lots.
