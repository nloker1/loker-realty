and I usually end up answering based on my day-to-day personal observations.

The RMLS provides great high-level regional data, but real estate is hyper-local. 
To understand what is actually happening on the ground here in Hood River and White Salmon, 
I extracted the raw MLS transactions to analyze our immediate monthly velocity alongside broader annual trajectories.


### 1. Months of Inventory (MOI)

Months of Inventory measures supply-and-demand leverage.
It tells us how many months it would take to completely exhaust all active property listings
if zero new homes hit the market. The calculation is straightforward: Total Active Listings ÷ Total Closed Sales.

**Context:** A balanced market sits between 4 to 6 months of inventory. 
Anything below 4 indicates a lean supply favoring sellers, while readings above 6 signal shifting buyer leverage.

**Key Takeaway:** 
April was a bit of an outlier, spiking to over 10 months of inventory. 
This happened because we had a wave of new listings land at the exact same time closed transactions
dipped down to just 8. Because we are looking at a smaller local dataset,
these temporary micro-spikes are completely normal.

Overall, the market is finding its balance.
Homes aren't flying off the market at the frantic pace we saw during the pandemic rush, but premium, 
well-priced properties still sell quickly. 
A lot comes down to the specific pricing strategy of the seller and their listing agent.

### Summary and Moving Forward 

* **For Buyers:** The spring seasonal opening brings fresh choices back to the board. However, underlying pricing parameters remain highly resistant to downward adjustments. Preparation, patience, and quick strategic execution are vital.
* **For Sellers:** 
The absolute inventory drought of mid-winter has officially broken. Overpricing your property in this environment will result in your listing stalling out, missing the seasonal buying wave, and accumulating high days on market (DOM).
